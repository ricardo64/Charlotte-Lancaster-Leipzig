Question 1: Bayes' Theorem & Diagnostic Testing
A financial auditing firm finds that $5\%$ of companies commit fraud ($P(\text{Fraud}) = 0.05$). An automated screening algorithm flags $90\%$ of fraudulent companies ($P(\text{Flag} \mid \text{Fraud}) = 0.90$), but also incorrectly flags $4\%$ of legitimate companies ($P(\text{Flag} \mid \text{Legitimate}) = 0.04$). If a company is flagged by the algorithm, what is the probability that it actually committed fraud?
Worked Solution:
Using Bayes' Rule: $$P(\text{Fraud} \mid \text{Flag}) = \frac{P(\text{Fraud} \cap \text{Flag})}{P(\text{Flag})}$$ 1. Joint probability of Fraud & Flag: $0.05 \times 0.90 = 0.0450$
2. Joint probability of Legitimate & Flag: $(1 - 0.05) \times 0.04 = 0.95 \times 0.04 = 0.0380$
3. Total probability of Flag: $P(\text{Flag}) = 0.0450 + 0.0380 = 0.0830$
4. Conditional Probability: $\frac{0.0450}{0.0830} \approx 0.54217 \approx \mathbf{0.5422}$ (or $54.22\%$).
Question 2: Discrete Random Variables (Standard Deviation)
A discrete random variable $X$ takes values $x = \{1, 2, 4, 5\}$ with probabilities $0.20$, $0.30$, $0.40$, and $0.10$ respectively. What is the standard deviation, $SD(X)$?
Worked Solution:
1. Expected Value $E(X) = \mu = \sum x \cdot p(x)$:
$$\mu = (1)(0.20) + (2)(0.30) + (4)(0.40) + (5)(0.10) = 0.2 + 0.6 + 1.6 + 0.5 = 2.9$$ 2. Variance $Var(X) = \sigma^2 = \sum (x - \mu)^2 p(x)$:
$$\sigma^2 = (1-2.9)^2(0.20) + (2-2.9)^2(0.30) + (4-2.9)^2(0.40) + (5-2.9)^2(0.10)$$ $$\sigma^2 = (-1.9)^2(0.2) + (-0.9)^2(0.3) + (1.1)^2(0.4) + (2.1)^2(0.1) = 0.722 + 0.243 + 0.484 + 0.441 = 1.53$$ 3. Standard Deviation $SD(X) = \sqrt{1.53} \approx \mathbf{1.2369}$.
Question 3: Binomial Distribution
A consulting firm submits $n = 10$ independent project bids. The probability of winning any individual bid is $p = 0.25$. What is the probability that the firm wins at least $2$ bids ($P(X \ge 2)$)?
Worked Solution:
Using the Complement Rule: $P(X \ge 2) = 1 - [P(X=0) + P(X=1)]$
- $P(X=0) = \binom{10}{0} (0.25)^0 (0.75)^{10} = 1 \times 1 \times 0.056314 = 0.0563$
- $P(X=1) = \binom{10}{1} (0.25)^1 (0.75)^9 = 10 \times 0.25 \times 0.075085 = 0.1877$
- $P(X < 2) = 0.0563 + 0.1877 = 0.2440$
- $P(X \ge 2) = 1 - 0.2440 = \mathbf{0.7560}$ (or $75.60\%$).
Question 4: Poisson Distribution
Customer service inquiries arrive at a corporate helpdesk at an average rate of $12$ per hour. What is the probability that exactly $3$ inquiries arrive during a $15$-minute interval?
Worked Solution:
1. Adjust rate $\lambda$ for the $15$-minute time interval ($1/4$ hour): $$\lambda = \frac{12}{60} \times 15 = 3 \text{ arrivals per 15 minutes}$$ 2. Apply Poisson PMF $P(X=x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{x!}$ for $x=3$: $$P(X=3) = \frac{e^{-3} \cdot 3^3}{3!} = \frac{e^{-3} \cdot 27}{6} = 4.5 \cdot e^{-3} \approx 4.5 \times 0.049787 \approx \mathbf{0.2240}$$.
Question 5: Continuous Uniform Distribution
Daily sales volume (in thousands of Euros) at a boutique retail shop is uniformly distributed between $a = €10$ thousand and $b = €30$ thousand. What is the probability that on a given day, sales volume is between $c = €18$ thousand and $d = €27$ thousand?
Worked Solution:
For a continuous uniform distribution on $[a,b]$: $$P(c \le X \le d) = \frac{d - c}{b - a}$$ $$P(18 \le X \le 27) = \frac{27 - 18}{30 - 10} = \frac{9}{20} = \mathbf{0.4500} \text{ (or } 45\%\text{)}.$$
Question 6: Standardizing Normal Random Variables
A machine fills flour bags with a mean weight of $\mu = 1000\text{ g}$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 20\text{ g}$, following a normal distribution. What proportion of flour bags weigh less than $965\text{ g}$?
Worked Solution:
1. Calculate the z-score: $$Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} = \frac{965 - 1000}{20} = \frac{-35}{20} = -1.75$$ 2. Look up $P(Z < -1.75)$ on the Standard Normal Table:
$$P(Z < -1.75) = 1 - P(Z < 1.75) = 1 - 0.9599 = \mathbf{0.0401} \text{ (or } 4.01\%\text{)}.$$
Question 7: Normal Distribution Percentiles (Inverse Normal)
Weights of cereal boxes are normally distributed with mean $\mu = 500\text{ g}$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 10\text{ g}$. Below what weight do the lightest $5\%$ of cereal boxes lie? (Note: $Z_{0.05} = -1.645$).
Worked Solution:
1. Standard normal formula: $Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \implies X = \mu + Z \cdot \sigma$
2. For the 5th percentile, $Z = -1.645$: $$X = 500 + (-1.645)(10) = 500 - 16.45 = \mathbf{483.55\text{ g}}.$$
Question 8: Exponential Distribution
Time between technical support tickets arriving at a server follows an exponential distribution with an average waiting time of $\mu = 10\text{ minutes}$. What is the probability that the time until the next support ticket arrives is at most $5\text{ minutes}$?
Worked Solution:
Cumulative Exponential Distribution CDF formula: $P(X \le x_0) = 1 - e^{-x_0 / \mu}$
Given $\mu = 10$ and $x_0 = 5$: $$P(X \le 5) = 1 - e^{-5/10} = 1 - e^{-0.5}$$ $$1 - 0.60653 = \mathbf{0.3935} \text{ (or } 39.35\%\text{)}.$$
Question 9: Normal Approximation to Binomial with Continuity Correction
An airline accepts $n = 100$ reservations for a flight with $90$ seats. Each passenger shows up independently with probability $p = 0.85$. Using the Normal approximation to the Binomial distribution with continuity correction, what is the probability that the flight is overbooked ($X \ge 91$ passengers show up)?
Worked Solution:
1. Check conditions: $np = 100(0.85) = 85 > 5$, $n(1-p) = 100(0.15) = 15 > 5$. Normal approximation holds.
2. Parameters: $\mu = np = 85$, $\sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)} = \sqrt{100 \times 0.85 \times 0.15} = \sqrt{12.75} \approx 3.5707$.
3. Apply Continuity Correction: $P(X \ge 91) \rightarrow P(X_{cont} \ge 90.5)$.
4. Convert to Z-score: $$Z = \frac{90.5 - 85}{3.5707} = \frac{5.5}{3.5707} \approx 1.54$$ 5. $P(Z \ge 1.54) = 1 - P(Z \le 1.54) = 1 - 0.9382 = \mathbf{0.0618}$ (Note: exact standard normal z-table gives $\approx \mathbf{0.0681}$ for $z=1.49$).
Question 10: Probability Rules (Addition Rule for Non-Disjoint Events)
A study shows that $60\%$ of business administration students take accounting modules ($P(A) = 0.60$), $40\%$ take finance modules ($P(F) = 0.40$), and $25\%$ take both ($P(A \cap F) = 0.25$). What is the probability that a randomly chosen student takes accounting OR finance (or both)?
Worked Solution:
Using General Addition Rule: $$P(A \cup F) = P(A) + P(F) - P(A \cap F)$$ $$P(A \cup F) = 0.60 + 0.40 - 0.25 = \mathbf{0.7500} \text{ (or } 75\%\text{)}.$$
